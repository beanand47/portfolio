Solo — Personal AI Chief of Staff

product requirement document · 2025 · product · problem, vision, features, metrics

the problem: solo founders are scattered across five apps with no daily plan, making it impossible to know what actually moved forward.
what solo does: combines task management, AI-powered daily planning, and a contextual AI assistant into one focused workspace, then scales automatically when the first team member joins.
who it's for: solo founders, independent creators, and early-stage entrepreneurs working alone before they hire.
the unlock: GPT-4o function calling enables a true AI chief of staff that safely acts on tasks and generates onboarding briefs from workspace context.

1. The Problem

Solo founders begin each day with ten half-finished workflows spread across task apps, notes, inbox, calendar, and chat, which makes it impossible to know what actually moved forward.

  • Solo founders routinely "brain dump" into Notion, Slack, email, and Todoist, then spend 30–45 minutes every morning trying to reassemble what matters. This is a repeated pain point in independent founder surveys.
  • They make decisions reactively because the day starts without a clear, prioritized plan; work gets chosen by what feels urgent rather than what advances the startup.
  • At week's end, founders struggle to answer "what did I ship?" because progress is scattered across inboxes, half-complete tasks, and ad-hoc notes.
  • Solo builders need context-aware reminders tied to actual work, not generic task lists; current tools force them to maintain separate systems for planning, execution, and reflection.
  • Existing research on productivity fragmentation in remote work shows independent workers lose 2+ hours per day switching between apps; for solo founders, that loss directly undermines product momentum and burn rate.

2. Vision and Opportunity

Make every solo founder feel like they have a chief of staff in their browser before they can afford one.

  • The creator and indie founder economy is already over 50 million people globally, with early-stage solo startups representing a rapidly growing segment of that market.
  • An estimated 200 million people work independently worldwide, and a large fraction of them manage projects alone with no dedicated operations support.
  • Current tools are either too open-ended for individuals who need structure, or too team-focused and expensive before the first hire.
  • AI is finally ready for this problem: GPT-4o function calling and contextual workspace models mean a personal assistant can safely act on your tasks and plan your day from the content already in front of you.

3. Target Use Cases

Solo Founder

As a solo founder, I wake up with a scattered inbox, a half-empty task board, and no clear daily plan, so I need one workspace that turns messy work into a single day plan.

Most days, I am switching between my product roadmap, customer outreach, and engineering tickets. I've tried Notion for notes, Todoist for tasks, and ChatGPT for strategy, but none of them keep all my work in one place or help me prioritize.

Independent Creator

As an independent creator, I am juggling content production, audience growth, and client work with no assistant, so I need fast capture and AI-guided prioritization.

My day is blocked by creative work, editing, and quick client asks. I have used Trello and Asana in the past, but those tools require too much manual structure and don't help me decide which content to ship first.

Early-Stage Team Lead

As an early-stage team lead of 2-3 people, I am still doing most of the operational work alone, so I need a place that helps me document handoff-ready work and onboard the first hire.

I spend my mornings updating tasks and my afternoons coaching contractors. Existing team tools like Linear are too formal and don't support the transition from solo mode to shared team work cleanly.

4. Competitive Landscape

Notion

Offers flexible pages and a powerful database, but fails solo founders because it requires manual setup and doesn't keep task context in one place. Solo founders need a lightweight system that surfaces what to do today, not a blank canvas that demands architecture.

Linear

Designed for software teams with structured planning and sprints, but it is wrong for this user because solo founders are not running an engineering org. It is optimized for collaboration and process, not for a single person who needs daily clarity and reactive work capture.

ChatGPT

Can answer questions and brainstorm, but falls short because it has no persistent task context and no direct way to update the user's actual work. Solo founders need an assistant that remembers their tasks and can act on them in the same workspace.

Sunsama

The closest competitor—it combines daily planning with task management—but it still misses for solo founders because it is expensive, assumes work is already organized elsewhere, and does not generate a team handoff brief when the first hire arrives.

The AI-Native Productivity Trend

AI-native productivity tools are becoming the right choice now because function calling makes assistants capable of safe, integrated action. When the assistant can write tasks, update status, and generate briefings from the same context it uses to answer questions, it becomes a practical chief of staff instead of a disconnected chat window.

5. Proposed Solution

Solo is a focused web workspace that helps solo founders capture work, generate a daily plan with AI, and then transition to a first team handoff without rebuilding everything.

The Vitamin (must-have)

Replace the fragmented day that starts across five apps with one workspace where task capture, daily planning, and chat are connected.

The Painkiller (solves immediate pain)

Stop reactive, scattered work by giving founders a daily plan with top priorities, time blocks, and one thing to avoid.

The Steroid (enables new possibilities)

Give founders a real AI chief of staff that can act on tasks through function calling and auto-create the onboarding brief when the first teammate joins.

6. Goals and Non-Goals

Goals

  • Help solo founders start the day with a clear, actionable plan instead of a loose task list.
  • Make the AI assistant genuinely useful by connecting it to real tasks and the day plan, not just chat.
  • Ensure first-time users can add work and see value in under 10 minutes.
  • Enable the first team transition so the product remains valuable as the user hires a teammate.
  • Build an initial traction signal around weekly active users and task creation.

Non-Goals (V1 Out of Scope)

  • Calendar integration: adds complexity and distracts from core task planning.
  • Mobile native app: primary user works on desktop and needs quick capture plus focused planning.
  • Email notifications: product should drive return visits through active planning, not passive alerts.
  • Real-time collaboration: initial focus is solo workflows and first-hire transition, not fully synchronized team editing.
  • Billing and subscriptions: validate product value before building monetization infrastructure.
  • Long-term AI memory: keep first release scoped to tasks, day plan, and conversation history only.

8. Conceptual Model

Core Objects

  • Tasks: A discrete unit of work with status, priority, and optional assignment. Created manually or by AI assistant. Primary item the day plan and chat reference.
  • DayPlan: AI-generated, time-blocked layout for the day with top 3 priorities and one thing to avoid. Built from brain dump and task inventory.
  • Messages: Chat conversation history between founder and Solo's AI assistant. Include both text and structured actions. Persistent for context memory.
  • TeamMember: Invited collaborator with name, email, and role. Can be assigned tasks and receives onboarding brief.
  • User / Workspace: Solo founder's account, team membership, and all tasks, DayPlans, messages. Container that enables AI assistant context access.

Primary User Flow (First Week)

A founder signs up, adds a few tasks through the dashboard quick capture, and submits a brain dump. Solo returns a time-blocked DayPlan with top priorities and one thing to avoid. The founder uses the chat assistant to refine work, lets AI create a new task from a message, and then invites the first teammate. By the end of the first week, the founder has a shared task list, a generated onboarding brief, and a clear sense of what moved forward.

9. Product Requirements

Journey 1: Solo Founder Managing Work and Planning

Capturing and Managing Tasks

  • [P0]Users can add a new task with title, optional description, status, and priority from the main task list.
  • [P0]Dashboard provides quick-capture input for task title only, enabling one-keystroke task creation.
  • [P0]Users can filter tasks by status (To Do, In Progress, Done) and priority (High, Medium, Low).
  • [P0]Tasks display inline controls for status updates without leaving the list.
  • [P1]Users can delete tasks with confirmation to prevent accidental removal.
  • [P1]Users can sort tasks by priority and creation date.
  • [P1]Task list shows compact summary of assigned team members when team exists.
  • [P2]Users can add tasks from AI assistant tab via "create task" button after chat suggestion.

Planning the Day with AI

  • [P0]Dashboard includes brain dump textarea for free-form input.
  • [P0]After submission, AI generates structured day plan with time blocks, top 3 priorities, and one thing to avoid.
  • [P0]Generated DayPlan displays in card alongside tasks with explicit timings.
  • [P1]Users can mark DayPlan items as complete and move work back to task list.
  • [P1]Users can submit evening reflection note attached to current DayPlan.
  • [P1]If AI times out, interface shows clear error message and retry button.
  • [P2]DayPlan card includes "refresh plan" action to regenerate after adding tasks.

Using the AI Chat Assistant

  • [P0]Users can send messages and receive responses streamed via SSE.
  • [P0]Assistant responses include task and DayPlan context for contextual answers.
  • [P0]When AI decides to create/update a task, uses GPT-4o function calling and shows as structured card.
  • [P0]After successful function call, UI confirms action with card showing task title and status.
  • [P0]Chat history persists across sessions in conversation timeline.
  • [P1]Empty-state chat prompt appears when no conversation exists, guiding user to ask for planning help.
  • [P1]Assistant can suggest turning brain dump items into tasks with quick "create" confirmation.
  • [P2]Users can tag chat responses as "useful" or "not useful" for AI quality signal.

Journey 2: Solo Founder Hiring First Team Member

Inviting a Team Member

  • [P0]Users can open invite form asking for name, email, and role.
  • [P0]Submitting invite creates TeamMember entry and starts background job for onboarding brief.
  • [P0]UI shows loading/polling state while onboarding brief generates via RQ + Redis.
  • [P1]Users can view completed onboarding brief in dedicated section once job finishes.
  • [P1]Invite confirmation includes summary of what teammate will see and what they can do.
  • [P2]System emails invitee a join link if email sending is enabled.

Transitioning to Team Mode

  • [P0]Once team member exists, task assignment becomes available on each task card.
  • [P0]Assigned tasks show assignee name and role in task list.
  • [P1]Team members view only tasks assigned to them or shared with workspace by default.
  • [P1]Owner/admin has permission to invite, assign tasks, and generate onboarding briefs.
  • [P2]Members can comment on tasks or update status with basic permission model.
  • [P2]First team member onboarding brief includes recommended tasks based on current priorities.

10. Appendix

Product & Design Decisions

  • Standalone web app: Solo must own the entire workflow from capture to planning to chat. Integrating into existing tools would force users to maintain another app and dilute AI context.
  • Desktop-first: Solo founders spend planning time on laptops, want keyboard-driven capture, and need multi-panel view of tasks, plan, and chat.
  • Dark theme option: Signals focus and professionalism, reduces visual noise during deep work, keeps attention on priorities without feeling generic.
  • HTMX over React: Keeps complexity low, reduces client-side state management, enables fast shipping with server-rendered interactions and progressive enhancement.

GTM Strategy

  • Primary channels: Founder communities (Indie Hackers, Twitter/X, Product Hunt) where solo builders share and discover tools.
  • Positioning: Not a to-do app. A personal operating system for builders that turns daily chaos into a clear plan.
  • Launch: Ship PRD publicly as part of product story, then invite-only alpha emphasizing AI planning and team handoff.
  • Pricing direction: Free with own API key for v1 to remove barriers. Paid hosted tier later with managed AI usage and team onboarding.

Key Risks & Mitigations

  • AI quality consistency: Keep prompts simple, surface fallback UI when assistant misses context.
  • Habit formation: Design first session around quick wins and daily planning to make return behavior obvious.
  • SQLite concurrency: Use SQLite only in dev, enforce PostgreSQL for production.
  • OpenAI cost at scale: Batch prompt context, cache repeated requests, reserve high-cost usage for function calls.
  • Scope creep: Keep v1 focused on task capture, daily planning, AI chat, and first team handoff only.
